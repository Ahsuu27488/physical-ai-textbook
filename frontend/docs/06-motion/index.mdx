Perception tells a robot what is around it. Motion decides what to do about it. Given a goal ("get to the kitchen", "put the cup on the shelf"), a robot has to find a path through a world full of obstacles, turn that path into movement that its motors can actually follow, and keep checking that nothing went wrong along the way.

This module has one idea at three scales. First you learn the planning algorithms themselves, on a grid you can read with your own eyes. Then you meet the two big ROS 2 frameworks that package those algorithms for real robots: **Nav2** for mobile bases and **MoveIt 2** for arms. Finally you zoom in on the last few centimetres of manipulation, **grasping**, where geometry, friction and learning meet.

## By the end of this module you can…

- Describe a robot's configuration space and explain why planning there is easier than planning in the real world.
- Implement breadth-first search, Dijkstra and A\* on a grid, and explain RRT and RRT\* well enough to know when to pick which.
- Bring up Nav2 with a simulated robot, tune its costmaps, and send navigation goals from Python.
- Use MoveIt 2 to plan, add collision objects to the planning scene, and follow a Cartesian path with an arm.
- Reason about what makes a grasp stable and sketch a complete pick-and-place pipeline.

## Module project

**Fetch it.** In simulation, make a mobile robot drive to a table (Nav2), then make an arm pick a block off that table and put it on a tray (MoveIt 2 and your grasp logic). You do not need one robot that does both yet; two simulations and a short script that glues the steps together is a fine result. Write down every point where the pipeline failed and what you changed. Those notes are the real deliverable.
